A word from the President

Dear visitors, partners and friends of Espérance's Hope,

It is with immense joy and deep gratitude that I welcome you to the official website of Espérance's Hope, the association fighting hydrocephalus and spina bifida.

The association is now 12 years old… How far we have come. For 12 years we have been trying, all together and as best we can, to keep it alive.

Creating this platform marks a new step in our commitment to give a voice, hope and a future to the children and families affected by these congenital malformations, which are still too little known. Our mission is clear: to raise awareness, accompany and support — medically, socially and psychologically — while advocating for better care and real inclusion of those affected.

Through this site, we want to share our work in the field, our humanitarian projects, our awareness campaigns and the stories of courage that inspire our daily fight. We believe that every life deserves to be lived fully, with dignity and love.

I warmly thank all our members, volunteers, partners and donors for their constant trust and commitment. Together, we make hope grow.

The road is still long, but I know I can count on each of you. We will get there: we will push back spina bifida and hydrocephalus.

My encouragement to all.

Welcome to our community of love, solidarity and light.

Espérance ALIA BROALET
President of the Espérance's Hope association
Neurosurgeon, specialising in hydrocephalus

Understanding hydrocephalus

Hydrocephalus is a condition in which fluid builds up inside the brain. The pressure rises and can cause a child's head to grow abnormally, vomiting, deep tiredness and delays in development.

With proper care it can be treated, most often with a small operation that drains the extra fluid. But across Africa, many families cannot get a diagnosis or treatment in time.

Early detection changes everything

The sooner a child is treated, the better their chances of growing up healthy.

Our mission

We want no child left without care because of distance, lack of information or lack of means.

  1. 01

    Raise awareness

    Inform families and communities so they can recognise the signs of the disease and challenge false beliefs.

  2. 02

    Improve access to care

    Help children get diagnosed and operated on in suitable health facilities.

  3. 03

    Support families

    Stand by parents before, during and after treatment, so that no one is left alone.
